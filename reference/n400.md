# Single-trial N400 amplitudes

30 participants read German sentences ending in an expected or
unexpected word (*Er trinkt seinen Kaffee / Schrank*). For each trial,
the mean EEG amplitude 300-500 ms after the final word at nine
electrodes. The N400 is more negative the less predictable the word
(lower cloze probability), and largest over centro-parietal sites. 80
items, two counterbalanced lists.

## Usage

``` r
n400
```

## Format

A tibble with 21600 rows and 9 columns:

- participant:

  Participant ID, `P01` to `P30`.

- list:

  Counterbalancing list, 1 or 2.

- item:

  Item ID, `i01` to `i80`.

- condition:

  `"expected"` or `"unexpected"`.

- cloze:

  Cloze probability of the final word.

- electrode:

  `"F3"`, `"Fz"`, `"F4"`, `"C3"`, `"Cz"`, `"C4"`, `"P3"`, `"Pz"` or
  `"P4"`.

- anteriority:

  `"frontal"`, `"central"` or `"parietal"`.

- laterality:

  `"left"`, `"midline"` or `"right"`.

- amplitude:

  Mean amplitude in microvolts.

## Source

`data-raw/n400.R`

## Details

True model:
`amplitude = 2 + p0 + i0 + e0 + weight * (-4 + p1) * (1 - cloze) + trial + e`.
The electrode weight is 1 at Cz and Pz, 0.9 at the other central and
parietal sites, 0.85 at Fz and 0.75 at F3 and F4; `e0` is a small
electrode offset. By-participant intercepts `p0` (SD 1.5) and N400
slopes `p1` (SD 1); by-item intercepts `i0` (SD 0.5); trial noise shared
across electrodes (SD 4) and electrode noise (SD 3). Cloze is 0.5 to
0.95 for expected and 0 to 0.05 for unexpected words. The midline
weights follow the N400 difference waves in Kutas and Hillyard (1980,
Experiment 2: -8.05, -9.62 and -9.24 microvolts at Fz, Cz and Pz); they
recorded no lateral sites, so the lateral weights are simulated. In
Kutas and Hillyard (1984), N400 amplitude tracked cloze probability
closely at central and parietal sites (correlations of 0.90 to 0.92 for
grand averages) and less at the frontal site (0.80). The sentences,
participants and amplitudes here are simulated.

## References

Kutas, M. & Hillyard, S. A. (1980). Reading senseless sentences: Brain
potentials reflect semantic incongruity. *Science*, 207(4427), 203-205.
[doi:10.1126/science.7350657](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.7350657)

Kutas, M. & Hillyard, S. A. (1984). Brain potentials during reading
reflect word expectancy and semantic association. *Nature*, 307(5947),
161-163. [doi:10.1038/307161a0](https://doi.org/10.1038/307161a0)

## Examples

``` r
pz <- subset(n400, electrode == "Pz")
aggregate(amplitude ~ condition, data = pz, FUN = mean)
#>    condition amplitude
#> 1   expected  0.589250
#> 2 unexpected -2.103867
if (requireNamespace("lme4", quietly = TRUE)) {
  m <- lme4::lmer(amplitude ~ cloze + (1 + cloze | participant), data = pz)
  lme4::fixef(m)
}
#> (Intercept)       cloze 
#>    -2.15742     3.73584 
round(tapply(n400$amplitude, n400[c("anteriority", "condition")], mean), 2)
#>            condition
#> anteriority expected unexpected
#>    frontal      1.57      -0.71
#>    central      0.84      -1.87
#>    parietal     0.44      -2.07
```
